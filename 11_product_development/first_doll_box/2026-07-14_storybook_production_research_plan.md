# Manu Storybook Production Research Plan

Date: 2026-07-14  
Status: Research plan; no supplier, price, safety, or final-format claim

## Purpose

Move the first Manu storybook from a strong 32-page product concept to an evidence-backed, supplier-ready production decision.

The research must begin with the child experience and the role of the book inside the first Manu box. It must then test physical options through printer input, dummies, proofs, samples, cost evidence, and family handling. No format should be treated as final because it appears in this plan.

## Decision to be made

Select a production route for the first Manu storybook that balances:

- the intended child age and reading mode;
- story pace and illustration density;
- durability and child handling;
- safe, credible material and finishing choices;
- fit inside the first-box architecture;
- print quality and colour consistency;
- feasible sampling, lead time, minimum quantity, and unit economics;
- repeatability for future Maitri companions.

## Current product and story baseline

### Confirmed strategic direction

- Manu remains the first complete companion and first-box proof.
- The first box stays focused on the doll, a 32-page character storybook, a letter from the character, six activities, and stickers.
- The storybook is the emotional engine of the box, not a secondary instruction booklet.
- Book 1 is child-centred and ends before queenship and war.
- The current working story is `Manu: The Horse Nobody Could Ride`.
- The story's central value is courage with patience.
- Accessories, extra outfits, a horse figure, playsets, journals, and later books remain outside the first-box baseline unless a separate founder decision changes it.
- Historical claims require review, and inspired scenes must not be presented as verified history.

### Working physical standard to validate

The current reusable launch baseline is:

- 210 x 210 mm square format;
- 32 interior pages plus 4 cover pages;
- saddle-stitched launch construction;
- 300 gsm matte-laminated cover;
- 140-150 gsm matte interior stock;
- full-colour CMYK;
- 3 mm bleed;
- 10-15 mm working safe margin;
- 300 DPI print-ready artwork;
- separate sticker sheets rather than bound sticker stock.

These are working specifications carried into research, not supplier-validated commitments. Printer imposition, grain direction, stock availability, binding limits, child handling, packaging fit, cost, and sample results may require change.

### Reconciliation required before layout

The earlier 32-page blueprint allocates 22 story pages, 2 letter pages, 6 activity pages, and 2 sticker pages. The manufacturable working standard moves stickers to separate inserts because sticker stock, adhesive, and die-cutting use a different production process.

Before pagination is locked, decide whether the 32 interior pages become:

- 22 story + 2 letter + 8 activity/reflection/parent-note pages;
- 24 story + 2 letter + 6 activity pages; or
- another 32-page allocation approved by the story editor and founder.

The separate sticker sheets must be specified and costed as their own component. They must not be counted as bound book pages without explicit printer evidence and a founder decision.

## Open decisions

- Final target age band.
- Independent reading, parent-led read-aloud, or a designed combination.
- Target reading time and final word count.
- Final story/activity/letter/parent-note pagination.
- Whether the letter is bound into the book or supplied as a separate keepsake insert.
- Whether activities are bound, perforated, or separate; perforation is not assumed.
- Final trim size and orientation.
- Cover construction and binding.
- Interior and cover stocks, coatings, inks, finishes, and certified material requirements.
- Corner shape and edge treatment.
- Final illustration style, density, and colour gamut needs.
- Sticker count, sheet size, face stock, adhesive, liner, die-cut method, and age/safety requirements.
- First-box internal dimensions, total weight target, and protection during shipping.
- Initial print quantity, reorder quantities, acceptable over/under-run, and inventory strategy.
- Budget ceiling, target landed unit cost, and acceptable sample/tooling cost.
- Required testing, labelling, declarations, and compliance review for the intended market and age band.
- Manufacturing location and supplier selection.
- Proofing, sampling, production, transit, and contingency timeline.

## Child-experience questions to answer first

- Who is the primary reader, and what reading ability can be assumed?
- Will a parent usually hold and read the book, or will the child handle it independently?
- Can a child comfortably hold the proposed trim size on a lap or beside the doll?
- How long should one sitting last?
- How much text can a spread carry without competing with illustration?
- Do the activities invite replay, or do they make the book feel like a worksheet?
- Should crayons or pencils be used directly in the book? If so, which paper/finish accepts them well enough?
- What happens to the book after repeated opening, bending, page turning, and storage inside the box?
- Which parts should feel like keepsakes, and which parts are intentionally consumable?

## Production routes to investigate

These are comparison routes, not recommendations.

### Route A: Square launch softcover

Working configuration:

- approximately 210 x 210 mm;
- 32 interior pages plus cover;
- saddle-stitched;
- coated or uncoated/matte stock options suitable for illustrated reading and the chosen activity use;
- separate letter and/or sticker inserts tested as variants.

Research purpose:

- test the current launch baseline for cost, lay-flat behaviour, handling, box fit, colour, durability, and repeatability;
- understand stock and finishing constraints at likely first-run quantities;
- establish whether the book still feels premium enough beside the doll.

### Route B: Premium durable square edition

Working configuration to quote with suppliers:

- similar square trim and story architecture;
- casebound or another printer-recommended durable cover/binding method suitable for a low-page-count children's book;
- rounded-corner and protective-finish options only where suppliers can evidence feasibility;
- separate activity/sticker components if the premium book should remain a keepsake.

Research purpose:

- test whether additional durability and keepsake value justify added weight, thickness, cost, sampling, and production complexity;
- understand the binding method a printer can reliably support for the final page count;
- compare shipping and box implications with Route A.

### Route C: Compact alternate trim for handling and box efficiency

Working configuration:

- a smaller square or portrait trim proposed by qualified printers after reviewing the story, target reader, and box constraints;
- 32 interior pages retained as the comparison baseline;
- softcover binding and stock options matched to child handling;
- separate inserts.

Research purpose:

- test whether a more compact book improves independent handling, box fit, shipping weight, and unit economics without weakening illustration impact or perceived value;
- identify layout compromises, word-count changes, and artwork reformatting costs.

Do not add a route only because a supplier sells it. Each route must answer a different child, product, or economic question.

## Evidence matrix

Record every route in one comparison matrix. Use quoted evidence, samples, or observed tests rather than adjectives alone.

| Reader fit | age, reading mode, hand size/handling, reading time | read-aloud and handling observations using a dummy | Founder + story/product lead |
| Story fit | words/spread, illustration area, page turns, activity allocation | paginated paper dummy and editorial review | Founder + story editor |
| Format | finished size, orientation, page count, spine/creep implications | printer specification and physical dummy | Product lead |
| Cover | board/stock, coating/lamination, edge/corner treatment | labelled material swatches and finished sample | Founder + product lead |

| Colour | process, profile, proof method, consistency tolerance | contract/colour proof and printer prepress guidance | Illustrator/designer + product lead |

## Printer RFQ package

Send the same base package to every printer so responses can be compared:

- project summary and intended child age, clearly marked provisional where open;
- route specification sheets;
- page count and provisional pagination;
- trim/bleed/safe-area assumptions;
- low-resolution sample spreads sufficient to show ink coverage and illustration style;
- expected book quantity and at least two useful price-break quantities;
- separate line items for book, letter insert, sticker sheets, packing, proofs, samples, tooling, freight, and taxes;
- delivery city/postcode and requested schedule;
- requirement to identify substitutions or assumptions explicitly;
- request for relevant physical samples and labelled stock/finish swatches.

## Printer questions

### Construction and prepress

- Which binding method do you recommend for this exact trim, page count, handling pattern, and quantity, and why?
- What page-count multiples, imposition constraints, creep, hinge, gutter, and safe-area changes should the artwork allow?
- What bleed, colour profile, total ink coverage, black construction, image resolution, trapping, and PDF standard do you require?
- How are proofs produced, and which proof represents the production result closely enough for colour approval?
- How will stock grain direction and book opening behaviour be controlled?

### Materials and finishes

- Which cover and interior stocks are currently obtainable and repeatable for reorders?
- Can you provide labelled swatches and finished children's-book samples using the proposed materials?
- Which surface works for the intended pencil/crayon interaction, and can we test it before approval?
- Which coatings, laminations, inks, adhesives, staples, boards, and finishes are proposed?
- What documentation is available for those materials, and what must be obtained separately?
- Are rounded corners or other edge treatments feasible at the proposed quantity, and what trade-offs do they introduce?

### Inserts and packing

- Can letter and sticker inserts be produced in-house or will they be subcontracted?
- What sticker face stock, adhesive, liner, die-cut tolerance, and packing method are available for the intended age/use?
- How will inserts be retained so they are not damaged or lost during packing and transit?
- Can each finished book/component be packed directly into the first box, and what handling charge or quality check applies?

### Quality and durability

- What tolerances apply to trim, registration, colour, lamination, scuffing, binding, and quantity?
- What in-process and final quality checks are documented?
- How are defects, shortages, and reprints handled?
- Can we review a white dummy, colour proof, and finished bound samples before the full run?
- What sample size is sufficient to assess production consistency?

### Commercial and timeline

- What is the MOQ and the itemised price at each requested quantity?
- Which one-time setup, proof, sample, plate, die, tooling, packing, freight, tax, and storage costs are excluded from the unit price?
- What is the quote validity period and payment schedule?
- What are the lead times for materials, proof, sample, production, packing, and dispatch after each approval?
- Which materials or processes are the main schedule risks?
- What changes at reorder: price, stock, colour, setup, MOQ, or lead time?

### Compliance boundary

- Which compliance documents can the printer provide for the proposed materials and processes?
- Which safety, labelling, age-grade, migration/chemical, small-part, or other tests do they believe apply, and who is qualified to confirm them?
- Which claims should Maitri avoid until independent review/testing is complete?

Printer answers are inputs, not legal or safety approval. Applicable requirements and claims must be confirmed by appropriately qualified parties for the intended market.

## Sampling and test sequence

1. **Paper pagination dummy:** verify size, reading flow, page turns, text density, activity space, and box fit.
2. **White production dummy:** verify trim, bulk, binding, opening, corners, inserts, and packaging interaction using proposed materials where possible.
3. **Colour proof:** verify illustration reproduction, skin tones, shadow detail, text contrast, and finish effect.
4. **Finished samples:** request enough units to see variation, not only one perfect sample. The current working trial range is 10-25 units, subject to supplier economics.
5. **Structured family handling:** observe reading, independent handling, repeated opening, activity use, and child/parent perception without making safety or sales claims.
6. **Recorded durability checks:** define a repeatable protocol for opening cycles, page pull/tear, staple or binding integrity, scuff/rub, light moisture exposure, crayon/pencil use, insert retention, and box transit simulation. A qualified reviewer should define any formal test requirement.
7. **Final preproduction proof/sample:** approve only after content, history, artwork, specifications, costs, and compliance inputs are closed.

## Cost model inputs

Compare routes using total landed and launch cost, not headline unit print price alone:

- editorial, historical review, illustration, design, prepress, and proofing;
- paper/board, print, binding, lamination/coating, special finish, and waste;
- letter and sticker production;
- dies, plates, tooling, colour proofs, dummies, and finished samples;
- component collation, quality control, individual protection, and first-box packing;
- freight, insurance, tax, duties if any, storage, damage allowance, and over/under-run;
- reprint setup, MOQ, stock substitution risk, and reorder lead time;
- book weight and its effect on the full box and shipping.

Record all prices with currency, quantity, date, tax/freight basis, validity, payment terms, and exclusions.

## Timeline inputs

The working schedule should show dependencies rather than announce a launch date:

- age/reading-mode decision;
- story and historical review;
- pagination and illustration lock;
- RFQ issue and response window;
- swatch/sample receipt;
- dummy and proof rounds;
- family handling and durability review;
- compliance input and any testing;
- final art/preflight;
- preproduction sample approval;
- production, QC, packing, freight, and contingency.

No public availability or delivery date should be derived until the selected supplier confirms a dated critical path after final specifications.

## Decision brief output

The research phase should finish with:

- a one-page recommendation labelled as a recommendation;
- the three-route evidence matrix;
- physical samples and test record;
- comparable RFQs and normalised cost model;
- recommended book specification and separate insert specifications;
- unresolved risks and required qualified reviews;
- box-fit and weight note;
- production timeline with approval dependencies;
- founder decision page: route, budget range, sample, supplier/next negotiation, and conditions.

## Exit gate

The storybook can move to implementation-ready specification only when Shwetika has recorded:

1. target age and reading mode;
2. final 32-page content allocation and insert treatment;
3. selected physical route after reviewing dummies/samples;
4. acceptable budget and quantity range using comparable written quotes;
5. selected supplier or a clearly authorised supplier-selection next step;
6. required safety/compliance review and claims boundary;
7. a dated production path with dependencies and contingency.

Until all seven are recorded, trim, stock, binding, finish, price, supplier, safety, and delivery timing remain provisional.
