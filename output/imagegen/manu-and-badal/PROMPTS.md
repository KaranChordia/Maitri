# Manu & Badal prompt set

## Shared art direction

Create a final full-bleed, ultra-wide 2.8:1 double-page illustration for the premium children's picture book **Manu & Badal: A Big, Brave Heart**. Treat `00-character-anchor.png` as authoritative: preserve Manu's face, age around ten, living-child proportions, dark wavy braid, indigo dress with tiny marigold motifs, burnt-orange sash and head wrap, and Badal's charcoal coat, inward-curving ears, gentle eyes, and white forehead star. Use the supplied book sample only for its heirloom Indian mood and ornamental language, never as a photographed-book composition.

Use premium hand-painted watercolor and gouache with fine ink, subtle miniature-painting detail, tactile paper grain, emotional clarity, and cinematic natural light. Keep key faces and horse heads within the left 48% and keep the center gutter free of important detail. Continue each environment across the right-hand page, then gradually reduce detail, saturation, and contrast into a warm atmospheric wash with enough calm space for only 2–5 short lines. The reading area must feel like the painted world fading into air, never like a placeholder. Use deep indigo, burnt orange, sandstone, marigold gold, soft Ganga blue, mango green, and cream.

Book 1 is childhood in Bithoor: Manu is active rather than royal, and courage is expressed as care before control. Avoid all embedded text, letters, numerals, framed text areas, scalloped panels, decorative borders around copy, hard masks, white holes, watermarks, book mockups, modern objects, crowns, heavy jewelry, battle, photorealism, 3D rendering, anime, doll/plastic skin, faces in the gutter, and clutter in the reading zone.

## Scene prompts

1. **Title/cover** — Dawn on the Bithoor ghats. Manu rests one hand gently on Badal's neck as they face the first golden river light together. Add a distant pavilion, lotus, marigolds, tiny birds, and soft hills. Let the river and sky fade gently into a pale text zone on the right.
2. **The courtyard rule** — Manu stands close to Moropant and leans forward with intense curiosity while children practice riding and wooden-sword forms. Badal waits at the stable edge; golden dust catches the light.
3. **Why not?** — Manu calmly questions a skeptical older courtyard woman while Moropant watches thoughtfully. Show a smooth river stone in Manu's open palm as her one-step promise.
4. **A horse named Badal** — Badal arrives powerful but frightened: tense legs, raised head, twitching ears, searching eyes. Others keep their distance while Manu alone notices his fear.
5. **A quiet answer** — Manu waits outside Badal's stall without reaching. His stamping slows, one ear turns toward her, and a warm shaft of light crosses the space between them.
6. **A difficult decision** — At blue-gold evening, the stable master tells Moropant that Badal may need to leave. Manu listens nearby, scared and thoughtful, holding her river stone.
7. **One chance** — Manu asks the adults for one chance to help Badal trust. Her chin is lifted and one hand is open; the stable master gives one restrained nod.
8. **A gentle beginning** — Manu brushes Badal's neck slowly. When he steps back, she visibly mirrors him and gives room. No pulling, forcing, or riding.
9. **Badal chooses** — Badal voluntarily comes to the stall door and lowers his nose to Manu's open palm. Manu is surprised and joyful but does not rush.
10. **Slow is still forward** — Manu leads Badal through one careful walking circle on a loose rope. Courtyard onlookers shift from doubt to wonder; circular hoof marks remain in the dust.
11. **The bravest kind of strong** — Manu rests a grateful hand on peaceful Badal while the community sees them differently. An elder smiles; rising birds and river ripples imply the future without showing war or queenship.
12. **Dear friend** — At Ganga twilight, Manu writes to the child reader with a reed pen while Badal rests nearby and one oil lamp glows. All scene paper remains blank.
13. **One kind step** — At sunrise, Manu ties the blank letter with orange thread and holds it to her heart. Stepping stones lead from shadow into light.
14. **Design your courage crest** — Manu works at a low table with a large blank shield-shaped paper, natural pigments, reed pens, stamp blocks, lotus petals, and a peacock feather. Badal peers through the stable window.
15. **Courage tracker** — Manu pins a blank strip bearing exactly seven empty circular medallions and places a marigold petal beside the first. Seven small clay lamps provide a counting cue.
16. **My big, brave heart** — At warm sunset, Manu and Badal sit beneath a flowering tree. A large blank heart-shaped paper, pigments, river stone, pencils, and a blank promise ribbon invite the child to respond.
