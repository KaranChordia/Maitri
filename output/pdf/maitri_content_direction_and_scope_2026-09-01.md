# Maitri Content Direction - Strategy, Monthly Scope, and Operating Plan

## Executive direction

Maitri's content should make children want to know the companions and make parents trust the care behind the stories. It should not begin as a high-volume product-promotion engine.

The recommended content model is:

> One approved Maitri source becomes a coordinated set of story, social, newsletter, parent, and short-video drafts. Human approval remains the release gate.

The content foundation already exists. The immediate decision is to activate it at a sustainable pace that fits the signed retainer and avoids expanding into heavy production without a separate scope.

## 3. Strategic content objective

For the next 30 days, content should achieve four practical objectives:

1. Introduce Manu as a friend before presenting her as a product.
2. Demonstrate Maitri's interpretation of courage through child-relatable moments.
3. Give parents a reason to trust and participate in the story world.
4. Invite interested families into the Maitri Circle without making unsupported product or delivery promises.

The objective is not maximum reach. It is a clear early signal of whether families understand the idea, respond to Manu, and want a continuing relationship with Maitri.

## 4. Audience and relationship model

### Primary adult audience

Parents and caregivers looking for culturally rooted stories, meaningful play, representation, and thoughtful parent-child experiences.

### Primary child relationship

Children should experience Manu as a curious, brave, kind, imperfect, and emotionally accessible friend. Content should invite imagination and conversation rather than deliver adult lessons in a child-facing format.

### Secondary audiences

- grandparents and family gift buyers;
- Indian families living outside India;
- educators, librarians, and beta readers;
- parents interested in history, values, and representation.

Secondary audiences should not dilute the first 30-day message. Manu and the parent-child relationship remain the centre.

## 5. Editorial promise

Maitri should offer families a reliable content experience:

- a story worth sharing;
- a child-friendly moment of courage, curiosity, kindness, or resilience;
- a parent prompt that opens conversation;
- clear separation between history and inspired fiction;
- one gentle invitation to continue the relationship.

Content should feel warm, specific, culturally rooted, intelligent, and emotionally honest.

It should avoid:

- generic empowerment slogans;
- motivational posting without a story;
- adult brand strategy presented as child content;
- sensational historical claims;
- treating battle as the only form of courage;
- unsupported claims about materials, safety, compliance, manufacturing, price, preorder, or delivery;
- hard-selling before the product and operating facts are ready.

Values should be experienced through scenes and choices more often than listed as abstract concepts.

## 7. Source-to-distribution workflow

Every content batch should begin with one approved source packet.

### Step 1 - Select the source

Choose one approved story scene, character insight, founder note, parent question, or validated audience learning.

### Step 2 - Define the claim boundary

Record:

- confirmed positioning;
- verified historical anchors, if any;
- inspired or fictional elements;
- approved product facts;
- unresolved or prohibited claims;
- approved CTA.

### Step 3 - Draft the derivatives together

From the same approved source, prepare the relevant combination of:

- website story or Letter from Maitri;
- Instagram carousel or static post;
- Instagram Reel script;
- matching YouTube Short script;
- parent prompt;
- waitlist or community invitation.

### Step 4 - Review once as a batch

The review packet should show the source, every derivative, visual direction, claim notes, and exact approval question.

### Step 5 - Publish and record

Record final copy, channel, date, URL, owner, source version, and approval date.

### Step 6 - Learn

Record useful response signals such as saves, shares, completion, replies, clicks, signups, questions, objections, and parent feedback. Do not treat follower count alone as proof of the strategy.

## 12. Review and integrity controls

Every public content item should pass the following checks:

- Is the source approved?
- Is the central story beat clear?
- Is the language suitable for the intended child and parent audience?
- Are verified history, interpretation, and inspired fiction distinguished?
- Does the item avoid unsupported product and launch claims?
- Does the visual direction avoid presenting concept art as a finished product?
- Is the CTA appropriate for Maitri's current pre-launch stage?
- Is final Shwetika approval recorded?

Higher-risk material should receive specialist review where required. AI output is never treated as final authority on history, child development, safety, compliance, legal matters, or brand judgment.

## 13. Measurement for the first operating cycle

The first month should measure learning rather than vanity.

| Signal area | Useful indicators |
| --- | --- |
| Character attachment | Saves, shares, story completion, repeated references to Manu, child or parent qualitative response. |
| Parent trust | Replies, questions, newsletter engagement, willingness to participate, and positive response to cultural and historical care. |
| Relationship growth | Waitlist signups, email interest, repeat visits, and participation in prompts. |
| Content usefulness | Saves, prompt responses, completion, clicks, and requests for more story material. |
| Objections | Repeated concerns about age, story fit, history, safety, price, product quality, availability, or delivery. |
| Operating quality | Approval turnaround, consistency, publishing completion, correction needs, and content reuse efficiency. |

At the end of the cycle, the report should answer:

1. Which content created the strongest meaningful response?
2. What did parents or families ask repeatedly?
3. What remains unclear about Maitri or Manu?
4. What should be repeated, changed, or stopped?
5. What is the next decision supported by the evidence?
