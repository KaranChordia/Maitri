## Purpose

This is the operating foundation for Maitri's digital presence. It is intended to make future editorial, creative, channel, community, publishing, and measurement decisions coherent before content volume increases.

This document does not approve a content calendar, captions, hashtags, publishing cadence, public historical claims, product claims, or launch promises. Existing draft calendars and channel assets remain reference material until the relevant foundation decisions and review gates are approved.

## Working language

Every item in this foundation must be marked as one of four types:

- **Decision** — approved and safe to use as an operating constraint.
- **Hypothesis** — strategically plausible but still requires approval or evidence.
- **Experiment** — a bounded test with an owner, duration, success measure, and decision it informs.
- **Future idea** — deliberately outside the current operating scope.

# Part I — Audit

## 1. The seven most consequential decisions before serious publishing

### D1. Choose the durable brand and relationship architecture

We must decide whether **Maitri** is the masterbrand and **Maitri Circle** is its relationship layer, or whether **Maitri Circle** is the public masterbrand. The repository currently uses both models. This affects every handle, bio, logo lock-up, page title, CTA, email sender, membership promise, analytics funnel, and future companion launch.

This is the first decision layer because every downstream system inherits its naming logic.

### D2. Fix the audience contract and first-stage age range

“The parent buys; the child must care” is clear, but the operational audience is not. The repository uses a broad parent segment around ages 5–10, while other character material reaches 9–12 and the first Manu reading level is not locked. We need one primary child range, one reading mode, one primary adult segment, and clear treatment of grandparents, NRI families, educators, and schools as secondary or future audiences.

Without this decision, voice, story length, participation design, video pacing, historical simplification, visual density, and measurement remain unstable.

### D3. Define the editorial promise and the boundary between story, history, and product

Maitri needs one explicit audience promise: what a family will reliably receive from the digital world before purchasing anything. That promise must sit above individual content pillars and specify the separation among:

- verified history;
- child-friendly historical interpretation;
- fiction inspired by history;
- character canon;
- product information;
- founder or workshop material.

The repository has strong claim guardrails, but they are not yet translated into a simple public-facing editorial contract or a complete approval matrix.

### D4. Select a small set of ownable editorial franchises

The brand has many useful ideas — Letters from Maitri, What Would Manu Do?, A Little Courage, Inside Manu's World, Storytime at Maitri, and The Circle Asks — but no approved franchise portfolio. We need to decide which formats are distinctive, repeatable, expandable to future companions, and capable of moving between social, Library, and email without becoming generic values content.

### D5. Give each channel one primary job and design the hand-offs

Instagram, YouTube Shorts, the website, Library, and email are named, but the system must specify where a story begins, where it deepens, where participation happens, and where a relationship becomes durable. The key decision is not merely which platforms to use; it is which channel owns each stage of discovery, depth, participation, and retention.

### D6. Establish the minimum viable publishing and approval operating system

The source-packet model is a strong base. It still needs named owners, review SLAs, historical review triggers, child-language review triggers, a visual asset-status system, a single approved source packet, a publication record, and a rule for withdrawing or correcting material.

The correct early cadence must be derived from actual review capacity, not from the existing 30-day draft calendar.

### D7. Define conversion, measurement, and the first 90-day proof

“Join the Circle” must offer a specific relationship benefit rather than operate as a generic waitlist. Measurement must distinguish:

- child attachment proxies;
- parental trust signals;
- relationship retention;
- product-interest signals;
- operational quality and review reliability.

Follower growth alone cannot prove the strategy.

## 2. What is already sufficiently clear

The following can be treated as established decisions unless Karan explicitly reopens them.

### Brand and business direction

- Maitri is story-first; the physical doll is one expression of the character universe.
- The immediate digital objective is emotional attachment to characters plus parental trust before product launch.
- Manu is the first complete companion proof and should remain the first reason to care.
- The first product direction stays narrow: one complete Manu box experience before accessory, playset, school, preorder, or wider-ecosystem expansion.
- Interest may be captured now, but payment, production, safety, compliance, price, and delivery claims require evidence and approval.

### Audience and emotional logic

- The parent is commonly the buyer, but the child must be won through story, friendship, imagination, and participation.
- Parent value should be felt through representation, cultural care, emotional intelligence, historical integrity, and thoughtful shared experiences.
- Child-facing material must not become an adult lesson disguised as a story.
- Manu's emotional territory is **courage with tenderness**: asking, trying, fairness, patience, kindness, friendship, and quiet brave choices — not battle as the default definition of courage.

### Voice and editorial posture

- The desired posture is a warm storyteller and thoughtful parent ally.
- The voice should be warm, intelligent, curious, culturally rooted, story-led, emotionally honest, gentle, imaginative, premium, inviting, and thoughtful.
- It should avoid academic, corporate, preachy, motivational, generic, nationalistic, excessively childish, or product-led behaviour.
- Values should be demonstrated through narrative more often than listed or explained.

### Integrity and governance

- The repository is the durable source of truth; project boards and chats are coordination layers.
- One approved source should feed cross-channel derivatives.
- Human review is mandatory for history, child language, product claims, founder claims, and public release.
- Verified history, interpretation, simplification, and inspired fiction must remain distinct.
- Concept art, prototype imagery, approved illustration, and final product photography must not be treated as interchangeable.

### Existing visual identity decisions

- The current website logo is the Flowing pages motif `23.svg`, used only as the logo mark with the Maitri wordmark.
- Josefin Sans is the website's single approved type family.
- The latest website instruction establishes pastel crimson `#ff96af` and pastel orange `#ffb098` as the official public palette. These supersede the older lead palette documented in `brand-assets.md` where the two conflict.
- The live website's visual logic is open, editorial, organic, softly layered, spacious, image-led, and free from repeated dashboard-like cards or hard dividers.
- Decorative motifs should be sparse, semantic, solid, and near-glyph-like; the logo mark must not be reused as decoration.

## 4. Contradictions and strategic risks

### R1. Maitri and Maitri Circle are currently collapsed in some public language

The new brief proposes:

- Maitri = brand and universe;
- Maitri Circle = relationship and community layer;
- Manu = first companion.

The current README and website instructions sometimes describe Maitri Circle as the overall story-first companion universe. This creates ambiguity: a family cannot easily tell whether it is encountering a brand, joining a membership, entering a story world, or doing all three under one name.

**Risk:** unclear brand memory, confused CTAs, unstable channel naming, and difficulty scaling future programmes.

### R2. Content production has already outrun foundation approval

The repository contains a 30-day calendar, drafted monthly letters, a public sample calendar, Instagram concepts, Shorts concepts, and content pillars. The new brief explicitly says not to begin with posts or a calendar.

**Decision:** treat these materials as an unapproved content inventory, not as a publishing commitment. They should be reclassified after the foundation is approved; they should not quietly define the strategy by existing first.

### R3. An internal Content Studio is currently linked from the public website footer

The Content Studio contains working strategy, pillars, channel drafts, parent prompts, and a sample publishing calendar. Its own language says “use this page before drafting,” which makes it an internal operating surface, not an audience-facing Library.

**Risk:** internal planning logic becomes public brand communication, unapproved work appears official, and the distinction between consumer story depth and production governance is blurred.

**Required later action:** decide whether the Studio is private/internal, a password-protected review surface, or deliberately transformed into a public creator/editorial-transparency experience. Do not assume the current public-footer placement is strategically correct.

### R4. Some existing content explains values instead of letting families experience them

Examples such as “five kinds of courage,” abstract values cards, brand-strategy explainers, and “the buyer is the parent, but the brand must win the child” may be useful internally. They are weaker as recurring public content because they move Maitri toward educational or motivational posting.

**Risk:** Maitri becomes another values account rather than a living character universe.

### R5. Multi-character visibility may outrun Manu attachment

The website now presents Manu, Nandini, Savitribai, and Kalpana, while the foundation principle says Manu should be the first complete proof before wider ecosystem development.

**Risk:** discovery is spread across a cast before one character has achieved emotional recognition. Future companions should signal that the system can grow, but should not compete equally with Manu during the first proof phase.

### R6. Historical fiction is structurally protected but not yet consistently legible to audiences

The repo has strong internal warnings, yet current draft story copy includes invented dialogue, scenes, feelings, and exact childhood moments. Internal labels alone do not prevent a parent or child from reading these as verified biography.

**Risk:** trust loss and accidental misinformation.

**Required later decision:** adopt a visible but elegant labelling system for “from the historical record,” “retold for young readers,” and “an imagined Maitri story inspired by.”

### R7. The repository contains an outdated palette note beside the current official palette

`brand-assets.md` documents a deeper rose and amber family, while the current `AGENTS.md` establishes `#ff96af` and `#ffb098` as official. Both may still be useful as an extended palette, but their authority is unclear.

**Risk:** inconsistent social templates and future creative work.

**Decision:** use `#ff96af` and `#ffb098` as the current primary pair; classify older colours as supporting tokens only after a formal visual-system review.

### R8. Audience age and reading mode are not fixed

The strategy broadly references ages 5–10, while companion material reaches 9–12 and production choices remain open.

**Risk:** inconsistent vocabulary, story pacing, illustration density, emotional complexity, parent prompts, and platform targeting.

### R9. The current publishing workflow is sound but may be too heavy if every asset needs every reviewer

The source-packet system correctly protects canon and claims. It does not yet separate low-risk fictional participation posts from high-risk historical or product claims.

**Risk:** either the system becomes slow and is bypassed, or review becomes superficial.

**Required later decision:** introduce risk-tiered review without weakening human accountability.

## 5. Recommended build sequence

The Digital Presence Foundation should be completed in seven decision layers.

| Layer | Decision | Why it comes here | Output released |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Brand and relationship architecture | Naming and belonging govern every downstream surface. | Brand hierarchy, channel naming rule, CTA language boundary. |
| 2 | Audience contract | Age, reading mode, buyer, and participation safety determine voice and formats. | Primary/secondary audience definitions and child–parent communication model. |
| 3 | Editorial identity | The audience promise and story/history/product boundaries define what Maitri publishes. | Positioning, personality, voice, writing rules, editorial and integrity principles. |
| 4 | Ownable formats and publishing grammar | Franchises and visual behaviour turn strategy into recognisable execution. | Territories, franchise portfolio, visual frames, motion/video principles, publishing mix. |
| 5 | Channel and relationship system | Once the material is known, each channel can receive a precise role and hand-off. | Channel architecture, website/Library/social/email relationship, participation and conversion model. |
| 6 | Governance and operating workflow | The approved creative system needs safe, repeatable execution. | Roles, review tiers, source packets, approvals, correction policy, production capacity. |
| 7 | Launch, measurement, and experiments | Targets should measure the approved system rather than arbitrary activity. | Launch narrative, 30/60/90 success criteria, experiments, measurement framework, explicit non-goals. |

Only after Layer 7 should the existing content inventory be accepted, rewritten, archived, or converted into a publishing calendar.

# Part II — Decision Layer 1: Brand and Relationship Architecture

## The question

What exactly is Maitri Circle in relation to Maitri, and which name should families encounter at each stage?

## Why this decision is consequential

The decision determines:

- the public brand name and memory structure;
- social handles and profile names;
- website title and navigation language;
- whether “Join the Circle” is a meaningful action or a decorative phrase;
- the sender identity of letters and email;
- the difference between consuming a story and becoming a member;
- how future companions and products inherit trust;
- analytics: audience reach versus relationship membership versus product interest.

## Option A — Maitri masterbrand; Maitri Circle relationship layer

**Architecture**

- **Maitri** — masterbrand, character universe, stories, companions, and product ecosystem.
- **Maitri Circle** — the opt-in relationship layer where families receive letters, participate, respond, save or explore deeper material, join early-reader opportunities, and stay connected.
- **Manu** — the first companion and emotional entry point.

**Public logic**

Discover Maitri → meet Manu → experience a story → join the Maitri Circle → build an ongoing relationship → express product interest later.

**Strengths**

- Creates the clearest long-term architecture.
- Makes “Join the Circle” a real conversion rather than a synonym for “visit the brand.”
- Allows Maitri to scale into companions, books, dolls, experiences, and partnerships without forcing every product under a community name.
- Gives email and participation a distinct promise.
- Matches the hierarchy proposed in the new brief.

**Trade-offs**

- Requires a later audit of website language that currently uses Maitri Circle as the overall universe.
- Requires care so “Maitri” does not feel cold or corporate while “Maitri Circle” holds all the warmth.
- Social naming may need an endorsed format during transition, such as “Maitri — home of the Maitri Circle.”

## Option B — Maitri Circle as the public masterbrand

**Architecture**

- **Maitri Circle** — public masterbrand, universe, community, and digital presence.
- **Maitri** — shorthand or product family.
- **Manu** — first companion.

**Strengths**

- Preserves more of the current homepage naming and existing public language.
- Feels warm, communal, and differentiated from a conventional doll company.
- Reduces immediate renaming work.

**Trade-offs**

- Collapses brand, universe, and membership into one concept.
- Makes “Join the Maitri Circle” semantically weak because the visitor is already inside it.
- Creates future ambiguity across products, retail, partnerships, community, and story experiences.
- Makes it harder to distinguish reach from membership and belonging from purchase intent.

## Option C — Equal endorsed architecture

**Architecture**

- **Maitri** and **Maitri Circle** appear together across most public surfaces, with one naming the universe and the other naming the community experience.

**Strengths**

- Can preserve continuity while the system transitions.
- Gives both names public visibility.

**Trade-offs**

- Places a cognitive burden on families at the moment of discovery.
- Encourages inconsistent usage across channels.
- Often becomes a temporary compromise that never resolves into a clean hierarchy.

## Recommendation

Approve **Option A: Maitri as the masterbrand; Maitri Circle as the relationship layer; Manu as the first reason to care.**

This is not merely a naming preference. It creates a scalable operating model:

| Entity | Owns | Does not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maitri | Brand belief, universe, companions, stories, product ecosystem, public trust | The mechanics of membership or a single character's identity |
| Maitri Circle | Ongoing relationship, letters, participation, family rituals, early-reader access, community listening, retention | The complete masterbrand or every public story experience |
| Manu | Character attachment, first story arc, courage with tenderness, first proof of the system | The entire brand promise or permanent limit of the universe |

## Proposed decision statement

> **Maitri is the story-first companion brand and universe. Maitri Circle is the relationship families choose to join. Manu is the first companion through whom that relationship becomes emotionally meaningful.**

## Consequences if approved

The next implementation work should:

1. Create a naming and CTA inventory across the website, social profiles, Content Studio, Library, email drafts, and waitlist.
2. Preserve the current public site until the copy changes are approved; do not perform a blind rename.
3. Define the Maitri Circle membership promise before editing the waitlist CTA.
4. Use “Maitri” for universe-level statements and “Maitri Circle” for opt-in relationship statements.
5. Use “Manu” for character-level stories, not as a substitute for the whole Maitri promise.
6. Track three distinct outcomes: Maitri discovery, Maitri Circle relationship, and future product interest.
7. Reconcile the README and website `AGENTS.md` after the public naming migration is approved.

## Provisional language model

This language is illustrative, not approved public copy.

- **Universe:** “Welcome to Maitri, a story-first world of brave companions.”
- **Character:** “Meet Manu, our first friend.”
- **Relationship:** “Join the Maitri Circle for letters, stories, and thoughtful moments to share together.”
- **Product interest:** “Be the first to hear when Manu's first companion experience is ready.”

## Approval required

Choose one:

- **Approve Option A** — Maitri masterbrand; Maitri Circle relationship layer.
- **Choose Option B** — Maitri Circle public masterbrand.
- **Request a bounded revision** — retain the three-level structure but change a specific role or public naming rule.

No work should move into Decision Layer 2 until this architecture is approved or revised.
