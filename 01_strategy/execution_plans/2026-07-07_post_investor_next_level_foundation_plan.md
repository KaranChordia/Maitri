# Maitri Next-Level Foundation Plan

Prepared for Shwetika review after the investor meeting

## 1. Why This Plan Exists

The investor meeting went well, and Maitri is now ready to move from a promising demo into a more serious build phase.

Shwetika's immediate direction after the meeting was:

- Two prototypes should start this week.
- Prototype work may take a couple of months, but should be expedited where possible.
- In parallel, Maitri should fully develop the characters, the storyline, and the social media presence.

This plan turns that direction into a clear foundation for the next phase.

The goal is not to rush into a launch. The goal is to use the prototype period wisely so that, by the time the physical product direction becomes clearer, Maitri also has a stronger story universe, clearer character system, and an active public presence.

## 2. The Phase Shift

### Previous Phase

Maitri was being shaped as an investor-ready and validation-ready concept:

- A pre-launch website.
- A Maitri Circle waitlist direction.
- A Manu-led story and first-box direction.
- Early social/content ideas.
- A clear story-first positioning.

### New Phase

Maitri should now be treated as an early-stage brand in active development:

- Physical prototypes begin.
- Manu becomes the first fully developed proof of the brand.
- The wider Maitri character universe starts taking shape.
- Social media begins creating audience familiarity before the product is ready.
- Every public-facing touchpoint should build confidence without making premature promises.

## 3. Core Positioning For This Phase

Maitri is a story-first companion universe where children befriend brave Indian heroines through stories, play, and meaningful parent-child reflection.

The doll is important, but the relationship with the character must begin before the doll is purchased.

For this phase, the brand should communicate three ideas clearly:

1. Maitri helps children meet Indian heroines as friends.
2. Manu is the first companion and the first complete story experience.
3. The product is being built carefully, with story, quality, and child attachment at the center.

## 4. The Three Workstreams

### Workstream 1: Prototype Development

Purpose:

Create two prototype directions that help Shwetika, investors, and future partners make better product decisions.

The prototypes should help answer:

- What should the first Maitri doll feel like in a child's hands?
- What level of visual finish, clothing, hair, and packaging feels premium enough?
- What parts of the product experience matter most to parents?
- What is realistic for production, cost, quality, and timeline?
- Which prototype direction best supports the Manu story and first-box experience?

Recommended prototype foundation:

Prototype A should focus on the strongest possible Manu companion doll direction.

Prototype B should test either an alternate doll style, alternate material/finish direction, or a more complete first-box presentation.

If the prototype definitions are not yet fixed, the immediate next step is to name what each prototype is meant to prove.

### Workstream 2: Characters And Storyline

Purpose:

Build the emotional heart of Maitri while product development is underway.

The first priority is Manu. Maitri should not expand too quickly before Manu becomes strong enough to carry the brand.

Manu should be developed across:

- Character personality.
- Story world.
- First story arc.
- Parent-child values.
- Key visual moments.
- Activity ideas.
- Letter from Manu.
- First-box story experience.
- Social media story beats.

The wider character universe can begin in parallel, but at a lighter level. The goal is to create a repeatable character system, not to fully launch many characters at once.

Each future Maitri companion should eventually answer:

- Who is she inspired by?
- What value does she help a child practice?
- What is her childhood story moment?
- What makes her feel like a friend, not only a historical figure?
- What would the child do, read, collect, or talk about with her?

### Workstream 3: Social Media Presence

Purpose:

Start building familiarity and emotional demand while the prototypes are being developed.

Social media should not behave like a sales launch yet. It should behave like a story-led community beginning.

The first social goal is:

Help parents and children understand why Maitri exists, who Manu is, and why children should fall in love with the characters before the doll is ready.

Recommended early content themes:

- Why Maitri is being built.
- Meet Manu.
- Courage can be gentle.
- Indian heroines as childhood companions.
- Story moments from Manu's world.
- Parent-child prompts.
- Behind-the-scenes prototype progress, only when safe to share.
- Maitri Circle invitations.
- Founder voice and values.

The tone should be warm, premium, child-safe, and emotionally clear. It should avoid sounding like a generic toy launch or a history lesson.

## 5. Recommended Two-Month Roadmap

### Weeks 1-2: Alignment And Setup

Decide:

- What the two prototypes are meant to prove.
- What Manu's first story should be called and centered around.
- What social channels should begin first.
- What content should be approved before posting.

Create:

- Prototype decision sheet.
- Manu character foundation.
- Manu first-story outline.
- Month-one social content plan.
- Updated website/waitlist direction.

### Weeks 3-4: Build The First Layer

Develop:

- Manu's character bible.
- First story beats.
- Parent-child prompts.
- Letter from Manu.
- First-box content outline.
- Initial social content batch.

Review:

- Prototype progress.
- Visual direction.
- What should or should not be shown publicly.

### Weeks 5-6: Make It Public-Ready

Refine:

- Manu's story language.
- Social captions and short video scripts.
- Website messaging.
- Maitri Circle invitation.
- Prototype update language.

Prepare:

- A simple public story calendar.
- A founder update format.
- A short investor/family update note if needed.

### Weeks 7-8: Review And Decide Next Moves

Evaluate:

- Prototype direction.
- Parent and audience feedback.
- Social engagement quality.
- Which story moments are resonating.
- Whether beta readers or parent reviewers should be invited.

Decide:

- Which prototype direction should move forward.
- Whether Manu's story is ready for deeper illustration or book layout.
- Whether the next character should be lightly introduced.
- What the next two months should focus on.

## 6. What Should Be Approved Now

To keep the work moving, Shwetika should approve or adjust:

1. The overall positioning:
   Maitri is a story-first companion universe where children befriend brave Indian heroines.

2. The phase priority:
   Build prototypes while developing characters, storylines, and social presence in parallel.

3. The Manu-first approach:
   Manu remains the first complete companion experience before Maitri expands too widely.

4. The social media posture:
   Begin public storytelling and community-building without making preorder, delivery, safety, or production claims too early.

5. The two-month working rhythm:
   Prototype decisions, Manu development, and social presence should move together rather than waiting for the product to be finished.

## 7. Guardrails

During this phase, Maitri should avoid:

- Announcing product availability before production confidence is higher.
- Taking preorder money too early.
- Making safety, material, shipping, or manufacturing claims before they are confirmed.
- Overdeveloping too many characters before Manu is strong.
- Turning the brand into only a history education project.
- Making the doll feel separate from the story.
- Posting social content without founder approval on sensitive product or history details.

## 8. What Success Looks Like By The End Of Two Months

By the end of this next phase, Maitri should have:

- Two prototype directions reviewed clearly.
- A stronger decision on the first doll direction.
- A well-developed Manu character foundation.
- A clear first Manu story direction.
- A first-box experience that connects doll, storybook, letter, activities, and stickers.
- A usable social media rhythm.
- A clearer Maitri Circle invitation.
- Better language for parents, investors, and early supporters.
- A foundation for deciding whether to move into beta readers, deeper book development, school pilots, or preorder planning.

## 9. Recommended Immediate Next Step

The first working session should answer three questions:

1. What exactly are the two prototypes, and what should each one prove?
2. What parts of Manu's story are approved enough to build public content around?
3. What should Maitri begin posting first so the brand starts building attention while the prototypes are underway?

Once these are answered, the prototype, story, and social work can move in parallel with much more clarity.
