# Maitri Identity and Visiting Card Brief

Date: 2026-07-14  
Status: Direction brief for founder review; not a final logo decision

## Purpose

Create a distinctive identity direction for Maitri that makes the brand feel like a warm, premium companion universe rather than a generic doll company, history project, or children's-content label.

The identity must express **companion + circle**:

- **Companion** means a child feels friendship, warmth, presence, and emotional closeness with a character.
- **Circle** means belonging, continuity, community, and a growing universe of stories and companions.

Manu is the first complete expression of Maitri, but the identity must remain broad enough to hold future companions. This brief authorizes route exploration only. No mark or lockup should be described as final until Shwetika selects a route and the selected route passes the usage tests below.

## Brand foundation

### Brand name

Maitri / Maitri Circle

The route study should show how the mark and wordmark behave when the public-facing name is written as `Maitri` and when the community/universe expression `Maitri Circle` is needed. The designer must not silently decide which form is canonical; that is a founder decision.

### Current positioning

Maitri is a story-first companion universe where children befriend brave Indian heroines through stories, play, and meaningful parent-child reflection.

### Role of the identity

The identity should:

- make friendship more immediate than product ownership;
- feel culturally rooted without reducing the brand to heritage decoration;
- carry both child delight and parent trust;
- support Manu now without becoming a Manu-only logo;
- work across a website, storybook, social profile, physical box, letter, and visiting card;
- remain recognisable before the wider product system is complete.

## Audience

### Primary buyer

Urban and semi-urban Indian parents of early-primary-age children who want meaningful, culturally rooted, premium-quality play. The final product age band remains an open founder and product decision; identity exploration must not turn a working age assumption into a public claim.

### Child audience

Children should experience the identity as warm, friendly, imaginative, and inviting. It should not feel institutional, educational in a school-like way, or designed only for adults.

### Secondary audiences

- Grandparents, aunts, uncles, and gift buyers.
- NRI families looking for an emotionally credible connection to Indian stories.
- Educators, librarians, cultural organisations, early partners, and investors.

The brand must win the child emotionally while giving the adult enough restraint, quality, and confidence to trust the experience.

## Desired emotional tone

The identity should feel:

- warm and companionable;
- premium but not precious;
- courageous with tenderness;
- contemporary and Indian without relying on decorative shorthand;
- story-rich, optimistic, and child-safe;
- emotionally clear rather than overly clever.

The best route should be able to sit beside a child-facing story moment and a serious founder or production conversation without looking out of place in either.

## Existing website visual cues to preserve

The active Maitri Circle site remains the visual authority for this stage. Identity exploration should learn from its current system rather than introduce an unrelated brand world.

Current cues include:

- **Typography:** Outfit across headings, body, navigation, buttons, forms, and the Story Universe interface. It is rounded, modern, friendly, and clean.
- **Primary warmth:** rose and deep rose, currently led by `#ef6f8b` and `#db5474`.
- **Supporting optimism:** amber/gold, currently led by `#f3a823`.
- **Broader universe colours:** teal `#189a93`, violet `#8b61c8`, blue, and green as supporting companion/world accents.
- **Light surfaces:** cream `#fff8ed`, soft blush `#fff1e7`, aqua, and warm gradient fields.
- **Dark surfaces:** plum and deep teal-black gradients with warm cream text.
- **Form language:** rounded consumer-facing forms, soft layered cards, organic image framing, gentle depth, and polished but playful motion.

The palette above is a starting constraint for testing, not an instruction to place every colour inside the logo. A strong mark should also work in one colour.

## Route exploration requirement

Explore at least three genuinely distinct identity routes. They must differ in idea and construction, not only in font, stroke width, colour, or ornament.

Each route should state:

1. the core idea in one sentence;
2. how it expresses **companion**;
3. how it expresses **circle**;
4. why it can scale beyond Manu;
5. how it connects to the current website;
6. what it risks or gives up;
7. how its symbol, wordmark, and combined lockup behave.

Possible territories to investigate, without treating any as the answer:

- a relationship-led symbol in which two presences create or complete a circle;
- a story/keepsake route in which a circle becomes an opening, page, embrace, or continuing path;
- a character-universe wordmark whose custom letter relationship creates belonging without needing a literal doll icon.

Routes that are merely three versions of a child silhouette inside a ring do not satisfy the requirement.

## Selection criteria

Score every route against the same criteria:

- The companion + circle idea can be understood without a long explanation.
- The identity feels proprietary enough to distinguish Maitri from toy, preschool, education, NGO, and generic women's-empowerment brands.
- It appeals to children without looking childish to parents.
- It supports a multi-character universe rather than one historical figure.
- It works with the existing Outfit-led website and rose/amber visual language.
- Its silhouette remains recognisable in small and single-colour use.
- It can be reproduced in digital, print, embroidery/label, packaging, and simple production contexts.
- It does not depend on gradients, tiny facial features, or fine ornamental detail to survive.
- It has a credible wordmark and not only a standalone icon.
- It feels emotionally warm before it feels historically grand.

## Required small-size and production tests

Do not approve a route from a large presentation slide alone. Show each shortlisted route in these conditions:

- favicon/app-icon crop at 16, 32, 48, and 64 px;
- website header at the current desktop and mobile navigation sizes;
- social profile circle at 40 px and a larger profile-preview size;
- one-colour black and one-colour white/reversed;
- low-ink grayscale office print;
- visiting-card preview at actual size;
- storybook spine/cover or box-label simulation at a realistically small scale;
- symbol alone, wordmark alone, and full lockup;
- light cream, rose, and dark plum/teal backgrounds;
- a simple production mock where thin lines and small gaps are likely to close.

The test is successful when the name is legible where it must be, the symbol remains identifiable, and the relationship idea does not disappear at the smallest intended size.

## What to avoid

- A crown, sword, fort, warrior pose, or queen silhouette as the main brand idea.
- A Manu portrait or Rani Laxmibai-specific symbol that cannot hold future companions.
- A literal navigation diagram or community-feature icon; “circle” is an identity idea, not a website function.
- Generic hearts, holding-hands stock symbols, school badges, NGO seals, or preschool mascots without a proprietary transformation.
- Overly ornate Indian motifs, mandala decoration, or royal detailing used as a substitute for a clear identity idea.
- Delicate line work, miniature faces, or several nested elements that collapse at favicon and print size.
- A mark that works only in the current gradient.
- A logo system that makes the website feel like it belongs to a different brand.
- Unverified claims such as “India's first,” product-safety language, or launch promises inside identity applications.

## Founder selection gate

Shwetika should receive one comparison board containing:

- the distinct routes shown side by side;
- one-sentence rationale and companion + circle explanation for each;
- actual-size small-use tests;
- one-colour tests;
- website-header and social-profile applications;
- the principal risk or trade-off of each route;
- a clear recommendation from the design lead, labelled as a recommendation rather than a decision.

The route is selected only when Shwetika records one of the following:

- approve one route for refinement;
- approve one route with named changes;
- request a controlled combination of specified elements from two routes;
- reject all routes with one consolidated reason.

After route selection, refine the mark, spacing, lockups, colour behaviour, and file system. Repeat the small-size tests before describing the identity as final.

## Visiting card dependency

Visiting-card design begins only after an identity route has been selected and its basic lockups have passed small-size and one-colour tests. The card must feel like a physical extension of the website: warm, restrained, premium, and clear. It must not become a second identity exercise.

Before artwork, confirm:

### Identity and purpose

- Which approved name appears: Maitri, Maitri Circle, or a defined lockup?
- Is the card personal to Shwetika, to another cardholder, or a general brand card?
- Is the primary use founder meetings, partners/investors, parents/community, suppliers, or a mixed context?
- Is one bilingual or alternate-language version needed?

### Cardholder information

- Exact cardholder name and spelling.
- Approved title/role.
- Phone number and whether WhatsApp should be signalled.
- Email address.
- Website URL and canonical domain.
- City, postal address, or no physical address.
- Social handles, if any are approved and necessary.

### Action and QR

- Should the card lead to the website, waitlist, founder profile, contact card, or another durable destination?
- Will the QR destination remain stable?
- Is a short printed URL required as a fallback?
- What tracking or privacy approach, if any, is acceptable for the QR destination?

### Physical specification

- Finished size and orientation.
- Single- or double-sided layout.
- Quantity and reprint expectation.
- Printer's bleed, safe-area, colour-profile, and file requirements.
- Paper family, weight, shade, recycled/FSC preference, and writing requirement.
- Finish options to quote: uncoated, matte lamination, soft-touch, foil, emboss/deboss, edge colour, or no special finish.
- Budget range and acceptable per-card cost.
- Whether finish choices must remain easy to reproduce across printers.

No paper, finish, colour result, or special process should be promised before printer samples and quotes are reviewed.

## Visiting card acceptance checks

- Uses the selected identity route and approved lockup files.
- Has a clear information hierarchy at actual size.
- Contains only confirmed contact details and destinations.
- QR code is tested from a physical-size print, on both iOS and Android where practical.
- Text, line weights, contrast, bleed, and safe areas satisfy the selected printer's specification.
- Front and back are verified in a print-ready PDF proof.
- Shwetika approves both content and physical finish before production.

## Exit gate

This brief is complete when Shwetika has approved the route-exploration criteria, confirmed the public brand-name usage to test, and authorised creation of distinct identity routes. The next gate is route selection. Visiting-card production remains blocked until that selection is recorded.
