# Maitri Story-to-Distribution System

Date: 2026-07-14  
Status: Working publishing system for founder approval

## Purpose

Make Maitri content a repeatable extension of approved story and founder source material, rather than a collection of unrelated posts.

One approved source should be able to become:

- a durable website story/article or Letter from Maitri;
- an Instagram carousel or static companion post;
- an Instagram Reel concept and script;
- a matching YouTube Short concept and script.

The derivatives may change length, pacing, framing, and visual treatment, but they must preserve the same story beat, emotional lesson, claim boundary, and invitation.

## Operating principles

- Maitri remains story-first and Manu-first.
- The child should feel friendship and possibility; the adult should feel care and credibility.
- The source is approved before distribution work begins.
- Derivatives are adaptations, not new canon.
- One story beat is better than several loosely connected messages.
- Historical facts, inspired scenes, founder statements, and product facts remain visibly distinct in the working record.
- No public asset should make premature product, safety, material, pricing, preorder, delivery, production, or proof claims.
- Cadence follows an approved template and review capacity; volume is not the first success measure.

## Source-of-truth model

Use the repository decision flow already established for Maitri.

### 1. Raw source context

Location: `10_source_context/`

Contains founder notes, supplied files, scans, reference material, and other original inputs. Raw source material is evidence or context, not automatically approved public copy.

### 2. Strategy and product decisions

Locations:

- `01_strategy/`
- `11_product_development/`
- `00_project_admin/` for current status and recorded decisions

These files define current positioning, product boundaries, launch posture, and what remains open. A public derivative must not outrun them.

### 3. Character canon and story source

Location: `02_character_universe/`

The Manu character bible and story files are the internal source for personality, emotional arc, story world, recurring values, and fictionalised scenes. Their review status travels with every derivative.

### 4. Approved source packet

Location: `03_content/` in a source-specific working folder or record.

Before derivative creation, create one source packet containing:

- source title and stable repository links;
- one-sentence story beat;
- child takeaway;
- parent takeaway;
- historical anchors and citations, if any;
- inspired/fictional elements;
- approved product facts, if any;
- prohibited or unresolved claims;
- approved CTA;
- founder approval date/version;
- historical/child-language/product review status.

The approved source packet is the distribution source of truth. If the canon or a product decision later changes, update the packet and assess every unpublished derivative. Do not silently patch only one channel.

### 5. Channel derivatives and publication record

Location: `03_content/`

Each derivative records its source-packet version, owner, review status, scheduled/published date, final URL or file, and any channel-specific adaptation. Published assets do not become new canon merely because they are public.

## Content status model

Use one shared status vocabulary:

1. `Source identified`
2. `Source review needed`
3. `Source approved`
4. `Derivatives drafted`
5. `Editorial/claims review`
6. `Awaiting Shwetika review`
7. `Approved / ready to publish`
8. `Published`
9. `Measured / learnings recorded`
10. `Archived or superseded`

Only `Source approved` material can move into derivative drafting. Only `Approved / ready to publish` material can be scheduled or posted.

## Approval workflow

### Step 1: Select one source

Choose one story beat or founder insight that supports the current Maitri phase. Record why it matters now and which audience it serves.

### Step 2: Build the source packet

Separate:

- confirmed fact;
- approved positioning;
- historical claim requiring evidence;
- inspired or fictional scene;
- product detail;
- founder quotation;
- recommendation or future idea.

Do not draft around an unresolved claim when the same emotional purpose can be served without it.

### Step 3: Complete specialist review

Apply only the reviews the source needs, but do not skip a required one:

- historical/citation review for names, places, dates, events, associations, quotations, and educational framing;
- story and child-language review for age fit, emotional safety, clarity, and tone;
- product review for materials, contents, safety, production, price, preorder, and timeline statements;
- founder review for public positioning, founder voice, sensitive history, product direction, and final CTA.

### Step 4: Approve the source packet

Shwetika approves the core message and claim boundary once. A named content owner then adapts it to the agreed channels.

### Step 5: Draft derivatives together

Draft the website, Instagram, Reel, and YouTube versions as one batch. This makes drift visible and reduces repeated approvals.

### Step 6: Run a cross-channel consistency check

Confirm every derivative carries:

- the same central story beat;
- the same historical/inspired distinction;
- the same product-claim boundary;
- an audience-appropriate version of the same invitation;
- no new fact introduced only for a hook.

### Step 7: Founder approval

Present one compact review packet rather than scattered messages. The packet contains the source card, derivative previews, claims checklist, CTA, and the exact decisions needed.

### Step 8: Publish and record

Record final copy, assets, URLs, dates, owners, and source version. Do not treat a scheduled post as published.

### Step 9: Learn without rewriting canon

Record qualitative responses, saves/shares, completion, clicks, signups, questions, objections, and child/parent feedback where available. Use these signals to choose future framing or tests. Audience response does not make an unverified claim true.

## One story to channel derivatives

### Source

One approved story or founder insight.

Example shape:

> Manu notices that a frightened horse needs patience rather than force. The emotional lesson is that courage can be quiet, observant, and kind.

This is treated as a Maitri story scene inspired by the character direction unless a historical reviewer separately verifies a factual claim. It should not be introduced as a documented childhood event.

### Website story/article or Letter from Maitri

Role: the durable, complete expression.

Suggested structure:

1. clear parent-friendly title;
2. a short child-facing opening scene;
3. the emotional turn;
4. what the story helps a child practise;
5. one parent-child prompt;
6. a transparent history/inspiration note where needed;
7. one current-stage CTA, normally Join the Maitri Circle.

The website version should be the best place to preserve nuance. It should not become a long content hub until the site's Stories/Letters architecture is approved.

### Instagram carousel

Role: a saveable visual retelling or value sequence.

Suggested 6-8 slide pattern:

1. one strong story-led hook;
2. scene/context;
3. tension or question;
4. Manu's noticing or choice;
5. emotional lesson;
6. child-facing reflection;
7. parent prompt or inspiration note;
8. Join the Maitri Circle CTA.

Keep each slide to one job. Do not compress qualifications into unreadable footer text; rewrite the claim or dedicate a clear final note slide.

### Instagram Reel

Role: an emotionally immediate 20-40 second story moment.

Suggested pattern:

- 0-3 sec: child/parent-readable hook;
- 3-12 sec: scene and tension;
- 12-25 sec: Manu's choice;
- 25-33 sec: emotional takeaway;
- final beat: simple invitation.

The caption carries the parent prompt, source/inspiration note, and CTA. Visuals and voiceover should not imply that concept art is a photographed final product.

### YouTube Short

Role: the same core short adapted for YouTube discovery and retention.

Use the same approved scene and claim boundary as the Reel. Adapt only what the channel needs:

- a clear spoken first line that works without caption context;
- self-contained narration;
- readable on-screen text;
- a title and description that do not add stronger claims;
- a concise final invitation.

Do not manufacture a separate YouTube fact, controversy, or sensational historical hook to increase reach.

## Claims and historical review rules

- Use `inspired by` when moving from history into a child-facing Maitri story.
- Do not present invented childhood dialogue or the exact horse plot as verified history.
- Separate a parent-facing historical note from the child-facing scene.
- Cite exact names, dates, places, relationships, quotations, and events before using them as educational facts.
- Do not use a motivational paraphrase as a historical quotation.
- Avoid battle, death, succession, colonial-law, or other heavy themes as a casual hook; use age-appropriate framing and qualified review.
- Do not state doll size, materials, safety, compliance, manufacturing origin, production status, price, preorder, returns, waitlist count, publication date, or delivery timing unless the relevant decision owner has approved documentary evidence.
- Distinguish prototype imagery, concept art, illustration, and final product photography.
- If a claim cannot be verified before review, remove or soften it; do not hide uncertainty in fine print.

## Tone and production rules

- Warm, premium, child-safe, emotionally clear, and story-led.
- Courage includes asking, trying, kindness, and quiet steadiness; it is not reduced to battle or triumph.
- Manu sounds curious and direct, not like an adult motivational speaker.
- Parent education supports the story instead of overwhelming it.
- Use Indian history personally and carefully, not as trivia bait.
- Every visual needs an asset status: source, approved, concept/reference, or final.
- Carousels need alt text; videos need accurate captions and a legible text-safe area.
- Use one clear CTA. At this stage, the default is interest capture, not payment or a delivery promise.

## First pilot source

### Proposed pilot

`Courage can be quiet and patient` - the Book 1 story beat in which Manu notices that a frightened horse needs attention and trust rather than force.

### Why this pilot

- It expresses Maitri's distinctive “courage with tenderness” positioning.
- It introduces Manu as a child and companion rather than a distant queen.
- It can speak to both children and parents.
- It supports a parent-child prompt without requiring a product claim.
- It tests the history/inspired-scene disclosure because the exact horse plot is part of Maitri's story world, not currently verified biography.
- It already aligns with the internal character bible, 32-page book direction, and existing month-one content themes.

### Pilot source dependencies

Before drafting for publication, Shwetika must approve:

- the exact story beat and wording;
- the intended age/read-aloud audience;
- the child and parent takeaway;
- the public wording that distinguishes inspiration from verified history;
- the CTA and whether the durable version lives as a website story or Letter from Maitri;
- the visual source that may be used.

If the required source approval is not available, the pilot remains an internal template exercise and must not be published.

## Pilot definition of done

The first pilot is done only when:

- one versioned source packet links to the current canon and decisions;
- historical anchors and inspired scenes are separated;
- story/child-language, product-claim, and founder reviews are recorded;
- the website/Letter, Instagram carousel, Reel, and YouTube Short all derive from the same approved beat;
- each derivative passes the cross-channel consistency and claims checks;
- visuals, captions, alt text, titles, descriptions, and CTA are complete;
- Shwetika approves the batch as one review packet;
- final files/copy and publication URLs are recorded in the repository;
- publication is verified on each intended channel;
- initial qualitative and quantitative learnings are captured after the agreed review window;
- the template is updated only for repeatable workflow improvements, not to rewrite the source story after the fact.

## Cadence gate

Do not commit to a high publishing cadence from the existing 30-day idea calendar alone. After the pilot, measure the real effort required for source review, visual creation, founder approval, accessibility, publishing, and learning capture. Then approve the smallest cadence the team can sustain without weakening story quality or claims review.
